Theory

This page states the method as optimization problems, gives every formula the code implements with the equation numbers of the paper, and states the properties of the method with the hypotheses they need. Equation numbers (n) refer to

S. Park and P. Van Hentenryck. Self-Supervised Primal-Dual Learning for Constrained Optimization. AAAI 2023. arXiv:2208.09046.

Equations labeled (B·) are the row-wise box form used by the code; each is shown to reduce to the paper's equation.

The parametric problem

The paper considers, for each instance (eq. (1)),

\[\min_{\mathbf y \in \mathbf Y} f_{\mathbf x}(\mathbf y) \quad\text{s.t.}\quad g_{\mathbf x, j}(\mathbf y) \le 0 \;(j \in \mathcal G),\quad h_{\mathbf x, j}(\mathbf y) = 0 \;(j \in \mathcal H),\]

with smooth, possibly nonconvex $f, g, h$. L2OALM writes every constraint, variable bounds included, as a box row

\[\min_x \; s\, f(x; p) \quad\text{s.t.}\quad l_i \le c_i(x; p) \le u_i, \quad i = 1, \dots, m, \tag{B1}\]

where $l_i, u_i \in \mathbb R \cup \{\pm\infty\}$ and $s = +1$ for minimization, $s = -1$ for maximization (so the method always minimizes $s f$). An inequality $g \le 0$ is a row with $c = g$, $u = 0$, $l = -\infty$; an equality $h = b$ is a row with $l = u = b$. For a JuMP problem the rows are L2OCore.dual_layout(problem) (see ALMFormulation); for an ExaModel they are the constraints followed by the variables.

The variable domain $\mathbf Y$. In the paper $\mathbf Y$ is "a closed subset of $\mathbb R^n$ defined by some bound constraints" (§3.2). It is never penalized or dualized: eq. (5) has terms only for $g$ and $h$, the dual network outputs "$\mu, \lambda$ for equalities and inequalities" (§4), and in the AC-OPF experiments the bounds are enforced by the output layer, a convex combination of the bounds with a Hardsigmoid (App. C.2). L2OALM follows this when the bound map enforces the bounds (enforces_bounds: SigmoidBoundMap, HardSigmoidBoundMap): the variable-bound rows then get zero multipliers in project_multipliers and contribute nothing to the losses (they are satisfied). With NoBoundMap the bounds are ordinary rows with multipliers and penalties — the paper's QP benchmarks have no variable bounds, so both choices agree there.

Multiplier convention. Row $i$ carries one multiplier $\lambda_i$ in the normal cone of $[l_i, u_i]$ at $c_i$: $\lambda_i \ge 0$ acts on the upper bound, $\lambda_i \le 0$ on the lower bound. The KKT conditions of (B1) are

\[s \nabla f(x) + \nabla c(x)^\top \lambda = 0, \qquad \lambda \in N_{[l,u]}\big(c(x)\big), \tag{B-KKT}\]

and $\lambda \in N_{[l,u]}(c)$ is equivalent, for any $\rho > 0$, to

\[c = \Pi_{[l,u]}\big(c + \lambda/\rho\big). \tag{B-NC}\]

For a row $g \le 0$ the paper's $\mu$ is $\lambda$; for a row $c \ge l$ it is $-\lambda$. JuMP.dual returns $-\lambda$ for both minimization and maximization (to_jump_duals); this is checked against Ipopt in the tests.

The losses

Primal loss — eq. (5)

With the dual proxy frozen, the primal proxy minimizes (eq. (5))

\[\mathcal L_p(\mathbf y \mid \boldsymbol\mu, \boldsymbol\lambda) = f(\mathbf y) + \boldsymbol\mu^\top g(\mathbf y) + \boldsymbol\lambda^\top h(\mathbf y) + \frac\rho2 \Big( \sum_{j\in\mathcal G} \max(g_j, 0)^2 + \sum_{j\in\mathcal H} h_j^2 \Big),\]

averaged over the batch. Row-wise (PDLAugmentation):

\[\mathcal L_p = \frac1B \sum_{k=1}^B \Big( s f(x_k) + \sum_i \big(\lambda_{ik}^+ (c_{ik} - u_i) + \lambda_{ik}^- (l_i - c_{ik})\big) + \frac\rho2 \sum_i v_{ik}^2 \Big), \qquad v_{ik} = \max(l_i - c_{ik}, 0) + \max(c_{ik} - u_i, 0). \tag{B2}\]

For $g = c - u$ the bracket is $\mu g$ and $v = \max(g, 0)$; for an equality it is $\lambda h$ and $v = |h|$ — exactly eq. (5). The paper writes eq. (5) per instance and notes that the losses "generalize naturally to mini-batches" (footnote 1); L2OALM averages over the batch. With $\lambda = 0$ it is the penalty loss of eq. (4) with all per-constraint coefficients $\rho_{g,j} = \rho_{h,j} = \rho/2$, which is the paper's remark that the zero-initialized dual network makes (5) identical to (4).

The alternative PHRAugmentation is the classical Powell–Hestenes–Rockafellar augmented Lagrangian

\[\mathcal L_p^{\mathrm{PHR}} = \frac1B \sum_k \Big( s f(x_k) + \sum_i \frac\rho2 \operatorname{dist}\big(c_{ik} + \lambda_{ik}/\rho, [l_i, u_i]\big)^2 - \frac{\lambda_{ik}^2}{2\rho} \Big). \tag{B3}\]

Its gradient with respect to $c$ is $\lambda + \rho\,\sigma$ with $\sigma$ from (B4): the primal step sees the multiplier one ALM update ahead. The two losses coincide on equality rows and on violated inequality rows ($c \ge u$). On satisfied inequalities they differ: (B2) keeps the linear term $\lambda (c - u)$, which keeps pushing the row inside, whereas the gradient of (B3) vanishes once $c \le u - \lambda/\rho$.

Dual target — eq. (6)

The ALM multiplier update of eq. (6), $\boldsymbol\mu_{k+1} = \max(\boldsymbol\mu_k + \rho\, g(\mathbf y), 0)$, $\boldsymbol\lambda_{k+1} = \boldsymbol\lambda_k + \rho\, h(\mathbf y)$, reads row-wise (dual_target)

\[\lambda^{k+1} = \Pi_M\big(\lambda^k + \rho\, \sigma(x, \lambda^k)\big), \qquad \sigma(x, \lambda) = c(x) - \Pi_{[l,u]}\big(c(x) + \lambda/\rho\big). \tag{B4}\]

For $g = c - u$: $\sigma = \max(g, -\mu/\rho)$ and $\lambda + \rho\sigma = \max(\mu + \rho g, 0)$; for an equality $\sigma = h$. $\Pi_M$ (project_multipliers) clips to the sign pattern of the row and to $[-M, M]$ with M = max_dual.

Dual loss — eq. (7)

The dual proxy is regressed onto the targets with (eq. (7))

\[\mathcal L_d = \|\boldsymbol\mu - \boldsymbol\mu_{k+1}\| + \|\boldsymbol\lambda - \boldsymbol\lambda_{k+1}\|,\]

implemented as (dual_loss)

\[\mathcal L_d = \frac1B \sum_k \Big( \|(\lambda_k - t_k)_{\mathcal G}\|_\varepsilon + \|(\lambda_k - t_k)_{\mathcal H}\|_\varepsilon \Big), \qquad \|v\|_\varepsilon = \sqrt{\|v\|_2^2 + \varepsilon^2} - \varepsilon, \tag{B5}\]

where $\mathcal H$ are the equality rows and $\mathcal G$ all others; with $\varepsilon = 0$, (B5) is eq. (7). The norms are not squared, as in eq. (7); the paper leaves the norm unspecified ("$\|\cdot\|$ represents an arbitrary norm", §3.1) and L2OALM uses the Euclidean norm.

Violation measure — eq. (8) — and penalty rule — eq. (9)

\[v_k = \max_{\text{training set}} \max\big(\|h\|_\infty, \|\boldsymbol\sigma\|_\infty\big), \qquad \sigma_j = \max(g_j, -\mu_j/\rho),\]

which is $\max_{i,k} |\sigma_{ik}|$ with $\sigma$ of (B4) (violation_measure). The paper prints $\sigma_j = \max(g_j, -\lambda_{k,j}/\rho)$ for $j \in \mathcal G$, i.e. with the letter of the equality multipliers; since $j$ ranges over the inequalities we read it as $\mu_{k,j}$, the multiplier of $g_j$ (as in Andreani et al., 2008, from which the rule is taken). Then (eq. (9), update_penalty)

\[\rho \leftarrow \min(\alpha \rho, \rho_{\max}) \quad\text{if}\quad v_k > \tau\, v_{k-1}. \tag{B7}\]

Algorithm 1

L2OALM.train! runs, for $k = 1, \dots, K$: (i) the primal phase on (B2) or (B3) with the dual proxy frozen; (ii) $v_k$ by (8); (iii) a frozen copy $\lambda_k$ of the dual proxy; (iv) the dual phase on (B5) towards (B4); (v) the penalty rule (B7). This is the order of Algorithm 1, lines 2–10: $v_k$ uses the dual network before dual learning, the dual targets use the current $\rho$, and $\rho$ changes only after dual learning. The rule applies for $k > 1$ (§4.3; $v_0$ is not defined), implemented as $v_0 = \infty$. The dual proxy's last layer should start at zero ("readily achievable by imposing the initial weights and bias parameters of the final layer of $D_\phi$ to be zero", §4.1), so that the first primal phase is the penalty method.

For reference, the paper's settings (App. C): $K = 10$ outer iterations; 500 inner epochs per phase (QP), 5000 inner iterations (QCQP), 250 inner epochs (AC-OPF); mini-batch 200; Adam with learning rate $10^{-4}$, decayed by 0.99 when the validation loss exceeds the best one; grids $\tau \in \{0.5,\dots,0.9\}$, $\rho \in \{0.1, 0.5, 1, 10\}$, $\rho_{\max} \in \{10^3, 5\cdot 10^3, 10^4, 5\cdot 10^4\}$, $\alpha \in \{1, 1.5, 2, 5, 10\}$.

Properties of the method

The following facts follow directly from the formulas above.

  • KKT points are fixed points. If $(x^\star, \lambda^\star)$ satisfies (B-KKT) for an instance $p$ and $\lvert\lambda^\star\rvert \le M$, then for every $\rho > 0$ the dual target (B4) returns $\lambda^\star$ and $v = 0$: by (B-NC), complementarity gives $c^\star = \Pi(c^\star + \lambda^\star/\rho)$, so $\sigma = 0$ and (B4) is the identity. If $f$ and $c$ are differentiable, also $\nabla_x \mathcal L_p(x^\star \mid \lambda^\star) = 0$ for (B2) and (B3): for (B2), at a feasible $x^\star$ the penalty gradient vanishes and the multiplier term has gradient $\nabla c^\top \lambda^\star$, so the gradient is the KKT residual; for (B3) the gradient is $s\nabla f + \nabla c^\top(\lambda^\star + \rho\sigma) = s\nabla f + \nabla c^\top \lambda^\star$.
  • Penalty schedule. With $\alpha > 1$, $\rho$ never decreases and never exceeds $\max(\rho_0, \rho_{\max})$.
  • Multiplier signs. Dual targets and completed multipliers have the sign of their row's dual cone and satisfy $\lvert\lambda\rvert \le M$, because $\Pi_M$ projects onto that set (and is idempotent).
  • Objective sense. The losses of $\max f$ and $\min -f$ coincide.
  • Variable bounds. With bounds = SigmoidBoundMap() or HardSigmoidBoundMap() the predicted $x$ satisfies its variable bounds exactly, by construction, and the variable-bound rows carry zero multipliers and zero dual targets, as in the paper.
  • Dual bound for strictly convex QPs. For a convex QP with $Q \succ 0$ the Lagrangian is bounded below in $x$ for every multiplier, so by weak duality the completed multipliers give a valid bound L2OCore.dual_bound $\le$ OPT. This is a property of the problem class, not of the method.

What the method does not provide. Feasibility of the predicted $x̂(p)$ (beyond variable bounds with a bound-enforcing map), optimality, and a valid dual bound in general are not ensured: the predicted multipliers satisfy sign conditions but not Lagrangian stationarity. Classical ALM convergence (Andreani et al., 2008, on which the penalty rule is modeled) concerns exact or approximate minimization per instance; it does not transfer to stochastic training of a finite network on sampled instances, and the paper does not claim it does: it states that the iterates "are expected to converge to the primal and dual solutions on the training instances" (§4.4), and proves no theorem about PDL. Measure feasibility and optimality gaps with L2OCore.evaluate.

Deviations from the paper

  • Box rows. One signed multiplier per row instead of separate $\mu$/$\lambda$ blocks; the formulas reduce to eqs. (5)–(8) as shown.
  • Multiplier clip $M$ = max_dual (default $10^6$), a numerical safeguard kept from the original implementation.
  • Smoothed norms in (B5) ($\varepsilon = 10^{-8}$), so the dual loss is differentiable when the prediction equals the target.
  • Epochs. Inner iterations $L$ are counted in epochs over the training set (primal_epochs, dual_epochs), as in the paper's experiments.
  • PHR option (B3), not in the paper.
  • Variable bounds without a bound map are dualized like any other row; the paper always enforces $\mathbf Y$ by the architecture (see above). Use a bound-enforcing map to match it.
  • Learning-rate decay of App. C.3 (×0.99 when the validation loss exceeds its best value) is not built into L2OALM.train!; the callback keyword can adjust the optimizer between outer iterations.
  • Norm in (7): Euclidean (the paper allows any norm).
  • Eq. (8): $\lambda_{k,j}$ for $j \in \mathcal G$ read as $\mu_{k,j}$ (see above).

Related methods

  • Kotary & Fioretto, Learning Constrained Optimization with Deep Augmented Lagrangian Methods, 2024 (arXiv:2403.03454): a single network predicts equality multipliers $\nu$; the primal point is recovered per instance by a box-constrained solve of the augmented Lagrangian, and $\rho$ grows geometrically per epoch. It would be another AbstractPrimalDualMethod on the same interface (it needs a per-instance inner solver, so it does not reuse L2OALM.train!).
  • "Analytical dual" PDL for AC-OPF: use $\mathrm{clip}(\lambdâ + \rho\,\sigma)$ inside the primal loss. Its gradient is the one of (B3), so it is available as augmentation = PHRAugmentation(); see Design.
